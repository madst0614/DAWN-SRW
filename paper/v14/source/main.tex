\begin{abstract}
Dense language models operate over learned high-dimensional states, but the
operations producing their transitions and the conditions governing their use
remain largely implicit. We ask whether learned transition computation can be
organized around explicit computational objects whose conditions of use and
contributions to state transition can be identified, measured, and intervened
on. We distinguish two computational roles and make them explicit through one
control--operation factorization: \(C(x)\) represents when, whether, and to what
degree a learned operation contributes, while \(O_i(x)\) represents the
transformation it performs. The current state induces this applicability
profile; composition combines the resulting controlled operation contributions
into a state transition. This motivates a \emph{State-Conditioned Program}: a
computational formulation of learned conditional operation
application. The
narrower architectural hypothesis is that transition-producing computation can
be organized into identifiable learned operations under explicit
state-conditioned control over their contributions. \DAWN{} provides one concrete
realization, using rank-1 read-write (RW) operators, an operation-address space,
normalized additive composition, full candidate
scope, and dense RW execution. At 393.8M parameters and 39.999B tokens, \DAWN{}
supports viability relative to the matched dense control: C4 cross-entropy is
2.9402 versus 2.9359 and the six-task zero-shot mean is 0.4629 versus 0.4597.
State-conditioned exact applicability support provides
evidence of conditional organization. A discovery-frozen
4,096-site Q/K-centered operator circuit passes suppression, four matched-control
families, and exact restoration on validation and held-out
indirect-object-identification (IOI) inputs, establishing causal accessibility;
fail-closed results delimit unsuccessful localization. They do not establish
formal operational or human-readable semantics,
benchmark superiority, hardware efficiency, or an intrinsic
or unique decomposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Language models repeatedly transform learned high-dimensional representations.
If \(X_\ell\) denotes the sequence representation at layer \(\ell\), a block
implements, at a coarse level,
\[
X_\ell\longrightarrow X_{\ell+1}.
\]
These transformations support powerful computation, but their internal
computational organization remains largely implicit. This does not mean that
dense Transformers lack state dependence or conditional structure. Rather,
their dense transformations do not natively expose a stable architectural
interface that separates the operations available to a state, the conditions
governing their use, and their individual contributions to the transition.

We therefore ask:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Can learned transition computation be organized around explicit
computational objects whose conditions of use and contributions to state
transition can be identified, measured, and intervened on?}
\end{quote}

To make this question architecturally testable, we distinguish two computational
roles: what transformation is performed, and when, whether, and to what degree
it contributes to the current state transition.

We make these roles explicit through one control--operation factorization:
\[
\underbrace{C(x)}_{\text{when / whether / how much}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\underbrace{\{O_i(x)\}}_{\text{what transformation}}.
\]
\(C(x)\) represents when, whether, and to what degree a learned operation
contributes, while \(O_i(x)\) represents the transformation it performs.
Language-model computation proceeds through evolving learned representation
states. At each transition-producing interface, the current representation is
the immediate computational state. We therefore make control explicitly
state-conditioned: the current state induces an applicability profile \(C(x)\)
over learned operations.
State-conditioned applicability modulates each operation response, producing
controlled operation contributions. An explicit composition rule specifies how
those contributions form the resulting state transition.

Together, we organize this computation as:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{current state}
&\longrightarrow
\substack{\text{state-conditioned applicability}\\[-0.15em]
\text{and learned operations}}\\[-0.1em]
&\longrightarrow
\text{controlled operation contributions}\\[-0.1em]
&\longrightarrow
\text{composition}
\longrightarrow
\text{next state}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This organization generalizes a familiar feature of program execution:
operations are applied conditionally to a current state. Here both the
conditions of applicability and the operations are learned, applicability is
continuous-valued rather than restricted to a discrete predicate, and multiple
controlled operation contributions may be composed into one transition.
Operational accounts such as Plotkin's provide limited conceptual lineage for
describing computation through explicit transitions between configurations
\cite{plotkin1981structural}; they do not supply the control--operation
factorization, a formal semantics for \DAWN{}, or a claim of formal equivalence.

We call this computational formulation a \emph{State-Conditioned
Program}: an explicit organization in which a current learned state induces
state-conditioned applicability over learned operations, controlled operation
contributions are composed, and a resulting state transition is formed,
\[
x\longmapsto\big(C(x),\{O_i(x)\}\big)\longmapsto F(x)\longrightarrow x'.
\]
Here \(x\) is the current representation state, \(C(x)\) is explicit
state-conditioned control, \(c_i(x)\) is the applicability of learned operation
\(O_i\), \(c_i(x)O_i(x)\) is its controlled operation contribution, and \(F(x)\)
is the composition of those contributions into a transition-producing result.
This use of \emph{program} requires no symbolic program text, discrete
instructions, human-readable predicates, or human-readable operator semantics,
and it does not define a formal structural operational semantics.

To constructively test this formulation in language modeling, we study the
narrower architectural hypothesis that transition-producing computation can be
organized into identifiable learned operations under explicit state-conditioned
control over their contributions. \DAWN{} is one concrete realization of this
architectural hypothesis. Here, identifiable means referable as a stable
architectural object whose contribution can be measured or intervened on; it
does not imply semantic purity, uniqueness, or an intrinsic decomposition. The
factorization makes applicability and individual operation contributions
explicit measurement and intervention interfaces.

The state-conditioned control design problem is to specify how a high-dimensional state
determines operation applicability. At the abstract level, this is an explicit
applicability profile,
\begin{equation}
x\longmapsto C_a(x)
=\big(c_{a,1}(x),\ldots,c_{a,N_a}(x)\big).
\label{eq:intro_control}
\end{equation}
Here, \(c_{a,i}(x)=0\) gives exact non-participation, while positive
values provide continuous-valued applicability. More generally,
applicability defines a learned relation between the current state and each
learned operation,
\[
(x,O_{a,i})\longmapsto c_{a,i}(x).
\]
This condition-like role is realized in the operation-address space: a
state-derived query and operator addresses lie on the unit sphere, and cosine
similarity measures angular proximity. A learned threshold bounds the query's
state-dependent angular neighborhood; addresses inside define the exact
applicability support, and continuous-valued applicability determines each
operation's degree of contribution. Learning jointly organizes persistent
operator addresses as type-specific spherical arrangements shared across states
and depth, with state-derived queries inducing local angular neighborhoods.
These contributions form the state transition; the exact
construction is given in Section~\ref{sec:operation-space}.

On the operation side, \DAWN{} uses a rank-1 read-write (RW) realization and
composes its controlled contributions as
\begin{align}
O_i(x)&=\langle x,\bar r_i\rangle \bar w_i,
\label{eq:intro_operator}\\
F(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{D/L}}{Z(x)}\sum_i c_i(x)O_i(x),
\qquad c_i(x)\in[0,1].
\label{eq:intro_field}
\end{align}
The read response \(\langle x,\bar r_i\rangle\) provides a signed state-dependent
scalar and \(\bar w_i\) its output direction, making each RW operator a directional
transition component. Each product \(c_i(x)O_i(x)\) is a controlled operation
contribution. \DAWN{} chooses normalized additive composition to combine multiple
such contributions into one transition-producing output. At a fixed state, this
preserves an explicit operator-wise additive decomposition; because applicability
depends on the state, the overall map \(x\mapsto F(x)\) is generally nonlinear.

\DAWN{} additionally reuses persistent type-specific operator pools across depth
and evaluates applicability over the full type-specific operator pool. These
identity and candidate scope choices, together with dense RW execution,
are implementation decisions rather than requirements of the formulation.

The empirical study tests this concrete realization under the evaluated
conditions through three ordered claims: viability, conditional organization,
and causal accessibility. Fail-closed results delimit where the mechanistic
localization procedure does not succeed; efficiency is not a headline claim.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A State-Conditioned Program formulation for
    transition-producing language-model computation and one architectural
    realization.} We formulate transition-producing language-model computation
    as a State-Conditioned Program. We then study the narrower architectural
    hypothesis that transition-producing computation can be organized into
    identifiable learned operations under explicit state-conditioned control over
    their contributions. \DAWN{} provides one concrete realization through persistent
    rank-1 RW operators, an operation-address space, normalized additive
    composition across Q/K/V and the residual-state transition (RST), full
    candidate scope, and dense RW execution.
    \item \textbf{A constructive empirical test with bounded evidence.} We test
    viability at 400M scale, report descriptive results for the 1.3B \DAWN{}
    checkpoint (1.272B parameters), provide evidence of conditional organization
    through state-conditioned exact applicability support, and test causal
    accessibility through a discovery-frozen IOI circuit confirmed by
    suppression, matched controls, and exact restoration. Fail-closed results
    bound where the same localization procedure did not succeed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{State-dependent and dynamically parameterized transformations}

State-dependent parameterizations have long precedents in nonlinear control and
neural sequence models. State-dependent Riccati techniques use state-dependent
coefficient representations \cite{cloutier1997state}, cited here as conceptual
lineage rather than mathematical equivalence. Multiplicative RNNs use
input-dependent transition matrices \cite{sutskever2011generating},
input-switched affine networks use input-dependent recurrent weights
\cite{foerster2017input}, and HyperNetworks generate weights for another network
\cite{ha2017hypernetworks}. Together these works establish state- or
input-dependent transformation as precedent. \DAWN{} studies the narrower
organization of transition-producing language-model computation into learned
operations and explicit state-conditioned control over their contributions.

\subsection{Conditional and modular computation}

Sparsely-gated mixtures of experts route among expert networks
\cite{shazeer2017outrageously}, Switch Transformers scale simple routing
\cite{fedus2022switch}, and ModuleFormer routes tokens over attention and
feed-forward modules \cite{shen2023moduleformer}. PEER applies product-key
retrieval to million-scale tiny experts \cite{he2024mixture}, while OmniMoE uses
vector-level Atomic Experts and structured routing \cite{shi2026omnimoe}.

Cross-depth sharing also has direct precedents: Universal Transformers recur
over shared layers \cite{dehghani2019universal}, MoEUT shares expertized
feed-forward and attention computation \cite{csordas2024moeut}, MoUE reuses a
layer-agnostic expert pool \cite{chen2026mixture}, and UniPool exposes a global
pool through per-layer routers \cite{huang2026unipool}.

Beyond feed-forward routing, SwitchHead routes attention-side experts
\cite{csordas2024switchhead}, UMoE shares experts across attention and FFN
computation \cite{yang2025umoe}, and Compositional Attention separates
query--key search from value retrieval \cite{mittal2022compositional}. These
lines establish state-dependent routing, fine-grained expert computation,
cross-depth reuse, and factorized attention as strong precedents. \DAWN{}
instead studies type-specific RW operators under state-conditioned control over
their contributions across Q/K/V/RST computation.

\subsection{Read-write operators and memory views}

Geva et al. interpret Transformer feed-forward layers as key-value memories with
input-sensitive coefficients over output directions
\cite{geva2021transformer}, and later decompose their additive updates into
parameter-vector sub-updates traced through vocabulary-space predictions
\cite{geva2022transformer}. \DAWN{} instead makes
\(c_i(x)\underbrace{\langle x,\bar r_i\rangle \bar w_i}_{O_i(x)}\) an architectural
operation contribution under state-conditioned applicability. Product Key Memory provides
lineage for factorized keys and structured retrieval \cite{lample2019large},
scalable memory layers extend content-addressable trainable memories
\cite{berges2024memory}, Fast Weight Programmers provide an outer-product
programming precedent \cite{schlag2021linear}, and MoRAM uses rank-1
associative-memory experts with intrinsic keys \cite{lu2026little}. These are
precedents for memory, retrieval, outer-product structure, and rank-1 experts;
\DAWN{} studies RW operators under a separate state-conditioned applicability
profile.

\subsection{Learned rules and dynamically composed functions}

Neural Production Systems instantiate learned condition--action production
rules: state- and entity-dependent matching selects rule--entity bindings,
learned rules update entity state, and the studies emphasize object-centric
structured dynamics and systematic generalization \cite{didolkar2021neural}.
Neural Interpreters factorize self-attention inference into reusable functions
represented by signatures and codes. Learned input types and signatures occupy
a hyperspherical type space whose matching controls function access; a shared
code-conditioned interpreter executes and dynamically composes function
streams across width and depth \cite{rahaman2021dynamic}. Neural Attentive
Circuits separate a circuit generator from its executor: signatures determine
module connectivity, codes condition module computation, and the executor runs
the generated configuration \cite{weiss2022neural}. These works provide concrete
precedents for learned conditions of computation: state- or entity-dependent
matching, type--signature compatibility, and generated modular configurations
determine which learned rules or functions become active.

\DAWN{} addresses the same broad problem of learned conditional access, but
gives it the explicit role of state--operation applicability,
\((x,O_i)\mapsto c_i(x)\), over learned operations.
Neural Interpreters and Neural Attentive Circuits use signatures and codes to
address or configure computation realized through shared interpreters or
modules. \DAWN{} instead uses persistent RW operators,
\(O_i(x)=\langle x,\bar r_i\rangle \bar w_i\), and derives each operator address from
that transformation's read/write parameters. It
applies this organization to transition-producing Q/K/V/RST computation and
operator-level measurement and intervention in an autoregressively pretrained
language model.

\subsection{Neural programming and programming interpretations}

Neural Programmer-Interpreters execute programs from supervised traces
\cite{reed2016npi}, Neural Programmer differentiably selects operations and data
\cite{neelakantan2016neuralprogrammer}, and Fast Weight Programmers connect
outer-product computation to instructions \cite{schlag2021linear}. RASP gives a
programming-language-level model of Transformer computation and realizability
\cite{weiss2021thinking}. Thus neural programs, operation selection, and
Transformer computation as program all have precedent. \DAWN{} instead studies
learned operations under state-conditioned control as native
transition-producing architecture, without symbolic source code or a
claim of formal semantic equivalence.

\subsection{Mechanistic analysis and causal intervention}

Mechanistic interpretability studies localized components through causal
intervention. IOI work isolates an attention-head circuit
\cite{wang2023interpretability}, AtP* approximates activation patching at scale
\cite{kramar2024atp}, and RAVEL evaluates localization against multiple causal
criteria \cite{huang2024ravel}. This literature distinguishes candidate
localization from causal confirmation. \DAWN{} supplies RW-operator intervention
targets, while its discovery freeze, suppression, matched controls, and exact
restoration test the specified IOI circuit without implying general semantic
interpretability.

Together, these lines establish precedents for state dependence, conditional
routing, learned functions, modular language-model computation, geometric
access, read/write decomposition, neural programs, and causal intervention.
\DAWN{} organizes these established roles within a State-Conditioned Program
formulation and tests one realization: RW operators under state-conditioned
control over their contributions, evaluated for viability, conditional
organization, and causal accessibility.

\section{DAWN-SRW}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{State Transitions and Control--Operation Factorization}
\label{sec:state-transitions}
\label{sec:transition-hypothesis}

Let
\[
X_\ell=(x_{\ell,1},\ldots,x_{\ell,T})\in\R^{T\times D}
\]
denote the sequence representation at depth \(\ell\). A Transformer-like block
produces state transitions through attention and a subsequent residual transformation:
\begin{align}
Y_\ell&=X_\ell+\operatorname{Attn}_\ell(X_\ell),\\
X_{\ell+1}&=Y_\ell+R_\ell(Y_\ell).
\label{eq:state_transitions}
\end{align}
The architectural hypothesis formalizes one way of organizing
transition-producing computation: identifiable learned operations are placed
under explicit state-conditioned control over their contributions. \DAWN{}
realizes this hypothesis through reusable RW operators, an applicability
profile, and normalized additive composition. For a token state
\(x\in\R^D\) and computation type
\(a\in\{Q,K,V,\mathrm{RST}\}\), define
\begin{equation}
F_a:\R^D\rightarrow\R^D.
\label{eq:composed_output}
\end{equation}
Here \(F_Q,F_K,F_V\) parameterize relational interaction and
\(F_{\mathrm{RST}}\) parameterizes a direct residual-state update. Rank-1 RW
operators, the operation-address space, persistence, and normalized additive
composition are realization choices, not requirements of the architectural
hypothesis. We
use \emph{operation} for the abstract transformation, \emph{operator} for an
indexed learned architectural object implementing an operation, and \emph{RW
operator} for the rank-1 realization used here.

\subsection{Read-Write Operators}
\label{sec:rw}

Each RW operator owns learned read and write parameters. With
\(\operatorname{unit}(z)=z/\max(\norm{z}_2,\epsilon)\), define normalized
directions \(\bar r_{a,i}=\operatorname{unit}(r_{a,i})\) and
\(\bar w_{a,i}=\operatorname{unit}(w_{a,i})\). The RW operator executes
\begin{equation}
O_{a,i}(x)=\langle x,\bar r_{a,i}\rangle\bar w_{a,i}
=\bar w_{a,i}\bar r_{a,i}^{\top}x.
\label{eq:rw_operator}
\end{equation}
The read direction produces a state-dependent scalar response and the write
direction its output direction, making this a rank-1 input-dependent
transformation. Q/K/V RW operators compose attention interfaces; RST RW
operators compose a direct residual-state update.

\subsection{State-Conditioned Applicability}
\label{sec:applicability}

For each computation type, the current state induces an applicability profile
over its \(N_a\) reusable RW operators,
\begin{equation}
C_a(x)=\big(c_{a,1}(x),\ldots,c_{a,N_a}(x)\big),
\label{eq:applicability_profile}
\end{equation}
whose coordinates determine whether and to what degree each RW operator
participates. Section~\ref{sec:operation-space} defines the state-derived query
\(q_a(x)\) and operator address \(k_{a,i}\). Their applicability score and
applicability threshold are
\begin{align}
s_{a,i}(x)&=q_a(x)^\top k_{a,i},\\
\tau_a(x)&=-1+2\sigma\!\left(xu_a+\beta_a\right).
\label{eq:applicability_score_threshold}
\end{align}
The evaluated \texttt{linear\_angular} rule defines
\begin{equation}
c_{a,i}(x)=
\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{s_{a,i}(x)-\tau_a(x)}
{\max(1-\tau_a(x),10^{-4})},0,1\right).
\label{eq:applicability}
\end{equation}
We call \(c_{a,i}(x)\in[0,1]\) the \emph{continuous-valued applicability}
of operator \(i\) at state \(x\). Its exact applicability support is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{I}_a(x)
&=\{i:c_{a,i}(x)>0\}\\
&=\{i:s_{a,i}(x)>\tau_a(x)\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:applicable_support}
\end{equation}
When \(s_{a,i}(x)\leq\tau_a(x)\), an operator contributes exactly zero; when
\(s_{a,i}(x)>\tau_a(x)\), \(0<c_{a,i}(x)\leq1\) varies continuously with the
applicability score. Thus the same applicability score and applicability
threshold specify exact non-participation and continuous-valued applicability.

\subsection{Normalized Additive Composition}
\label{sec:operator-composition}

\DAWN{} uses normalized additive composition to combine controlled RW-operator
contributions into the output \(F_a(x)\). With normalization factor \(Z_a(x)\),
\begin{align}
Z_a(x)&=\left[\max\!\left(\sum_i c_{a,i}(x),1\right)\right]^{p_a},\\
F_a(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{D/L}}{Z_a(x)}
\sum_i c_{a,i}(x)\bar w_{a,i}\bar r_{a,i}^{\top}x.
\label{eq:implemented_composition}
\end{align}
In each term, \(c_{a,i}(x)\) specifies state-conditioned applicability and
\(\bar w_{a,i}\bar r_{a,i}^{\top}x\) is the operation response; their product is
a controlled operation contribution. This additive form is a deliberate
realization choice: at a fixed state, it preserves an explicit operator-wise
decomposition of the transition-producing output, making individual controlled
contributions directly measurable and available as intervention targets; the
reported interventions suppress or restore selected numerator terms while
leaving the normalization factor unchanged. A nonlinear mixer would generally
introduce cross-operation interactions that obscure this exact decomposition;
because both \(c_{a,i}(x)\) and \(Z_a(x)\) are state-dependent, the overall mapping
\(x\mapsto F_a(x)\) remains generally nonlinear. Exact composition parameters
are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:equations}.

\subsection{Operation-Address Space}
\label{sec:operation-space}

In this realization, evaluating the condition under which an operation
contributes becomes a learned geometric relation between the current state and
that operator. The operation-address space provides one geometric realization
of state--operation applicability. Representation and RW computation
remain in \(\R^D\); the query and operator addresses
\(q_a(x),k_{a,i}\in\R^{d_c}\) are control variables. Here, \(d_c=256\) and
\begin{equation}
q_a(x)=\operatorname{unit}(xP_a+b_a)\in\R^{d_c}.
\label{eq:query}
\end{equation}
A shared probe pair \(P_r,P_w\in\R^{D\times d_c}\) maps functional read and
write directions into the operator address
\begin{equation}
k_{a,i}=\operatorname{unit}\!\left(
\operatorname{unit}(\bar r_{a,i}^{\top}P_r)\od
\operatorname{unit}(\bar w_{a,i}^{\top}P_w)\right)\in\R^{d_c}.
\label{eq:rw_address}
\end{equation}
Each operator address is a learned function of what its RW operator reads and
where it writes, rather than an independently parameterized address.

Because both \(q_a(x)\) and \(k_{a,i}\) are unit-normalized, they lie on the
unit sphere \(\mathbb{S}^{d_c-1}\). Writing \(\theta_{a,i}(x)\) for the angle
between them, \(s_{a,i}(x)=q_a(x)^\top k_{a,i}=\cos\!\big(\theta_{a,i}(x)\big)\),
so \(s_{a,i}(x)>\tau_a(x)\) is equivalent to
\(\theta_{a,i}(x)<\arccos\!\big(\tau_a(x)\big)\). Thus the exact applicability
support corresponds geometrically to a state-dependent spherical cap, or
angular neighborhood, centered at \(q_a(x)\) with angular radius
\(\arccos\!\big(\tau_a(x)\big)\). Within this neighborhood,
\(c_{a,i}(x)\) gives continuous-valued applicability that varies with the cosine
applicability score above the threshold and determines to what degree the
operator contributes. This is the spherical realization of
\((x,O_{a,i})\mapsto c_{a,i}(x)\); exact implementation details are restated in
Appendix~\ref{app:equations}.

\subsection{Attention-Mediated and Residual Transitions}
\label{sec:block}

For \(\widetilde X_\ell=\operatorname{LN}^{(1)}_\ell(X_\ell)\), three
normalized additive compositions produce the attention interfaces:
\begin{align}
Q_\ell&=F_Q(\widetilde X_\ell),\nonumber\\
K_\ell&=F_K(\widetilde X_\ell),\nonumber\\
V_\ell&=F_V(\widetilde X_\ell),\nonumber\\
A_\ell&=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
\frac{Q_\ell K_\ell^\top}{\sqrt{d_h}}+M_{\mathrm{causal}}\right)
\!,\nonumber\\
\Delta X_{\mathrm{attn},\ell}&=A_\ell V_\ell W^O_\ell,\\
Y_\ell&=X_\ell+\Delta X_{\mathrm{attn},\ell}.
\label{eq:attention}
\end{align}
Q and K use a shared type-specific operator pool, while V uses a separate
operator pool. A second
normalization \(\widetilde Y_\ell=\operatorname{LN}^{(2)}_\ell(Y_\ell)\) then
queries the RST operator pool:
\begin{equation}
X_{\ell+1}=Y_\ell+F_{\mathrm{RST}}(\widetilde Y_\ell).
\label{eq:rst}
\end{equation}
Thus RST applicability is evaluated from the contextualized post-attention
state, and its normalized additive composition produces the direct
residual-state update.

\subsection{Cross-Depth Operator Reuse and Shared Control}
\label{sec:reuse}

Type-specific operator pools and their state-conditioned control parameterizations
are shared across all \(L\) layers. Evolving normalized states therefore query
the same operator pools rather than a separately parameterized profile at each
depth. The two
LayerNorms and attention output projection \(W^O_\ell\) remain layer-specific;
Appendix~\ref{app:settings} gives the complete sharing inventory.

\subsection{Candidate Scope and Physical Execution}
\label{sec:candidate-execution}

For computation type \(a\), the candidate scope is the full type-specific
operator pool. Thus
\[
\mathcal{I}_a(x)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N_a\},
\]
with all \(N_a\) operators considered as candidates before applicability is
determined. Applicability support is generally a strict subset, while physical
RW execution is dense. Sharding, chunking, and accounting details appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:compute}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the results establish}

The constructive test supports three ordered claims. First, the matched 400M
comparison establishes viability under the measured protocols. Second,
state-conditioned exact applicability support provides evidence of conditional
organization in the evaluated model; candidate scope is the full type-specific
operator pool and physical RW execution is dense. Third, the discovery-frozen
IOI circuit establishes causal
accessibility: suppression changes behavior, matched controls do not reproduce
the effect, and exact same-example restoration recovers it on independent
validation and held-out data. Fail-closed results on RAVEL, ARC, and 1.3B IOI
delimit where this localization procedure did not succeed.

These findings support one explicit realization under the evaluated conditions,
not an intrinsic or unique decomposition. Their practical consequence is an
interface in which learned operations are referable, their state-conditioned
contributions measurable, and selected contributions available for intervention.

\subsection{From the Computational Question to an Open Design Space}

The State-Conditioned Program formulation opens four design axes: control and
applicability, operation and identity, coupling and composition, and candidate
scope and physical execution. \DAWN{} supplies one tested point---rank-1 RW
operators, an
operation-address space, cross-depth persistence, full candidate scope, and
normalized additive composition---rather than requirements for the formulation.

\paragraph{Control and applicability.}
How should state--operation applicability be represented? \DAWN{} derives
\(C_a(x)\) in a learned \(d_c=256\) operation-address space. Alternatives could
use functional parameters, intrinsic read activation, separate control
representations, or no geometry. Open questions concern intrinsic control
dimension, locality, and whether geometric similarity corresponds to functional
similarity; the present evidence establishes neither that 256 is intrinsic nor
that one organization is preferable.

\paragraph{Operation and identity.}
What should constitute a reusable learned operation? Rank-1 RW operators are
one granularity; alternatives could be finer, coarser, structured, or
hierarchical. \DAWN{} persists type-specific identities across depth, while other
scopes could be layer-local or recurrent. Persistence makes stability measurable
but does not guarantee a single function or semantic purity; operations may
remain distributed or polysemantic.

\newpage
\paragraph{Coupling and composition.}
After control and operations are made explicit, how should their controlled
contributions combine into a transition? In ordinary single-branch discrete
conditional execution, recomposition is effectively trivial; when
state-conditioned applicability is continuous-valued, multiple operations may contribute
simultaneously, making composition an explicit design problem. \DAWN{} derives addresses from
read/write parameters without identifying control variables with RW operations,
multiplies each operation response by its state-conditioned applicability, and
then uses normalized additive composition, partly because the additive form
preserves explicit operator-wise contributions. Alternative nonlinear
composition rules may trade this separability for different cross-operation
interaction structure; other designs could also couple the roles differently or
compose contributions sequentially, hierarchically, recurrently, or otherwise.

\paragraph{Candidate scope and physical execution.}
How much of an operator pool should candidate scope include? \DAWN{} uses the full
type-specific operator pool; restricted or state-dependent candidate scope
remains an open empirical question.

\subsection{From Applicability Support to Physical Execution}
\label{sec:future-execution}

For a given representation state, many operators have exactly zero
applicability, but this does not establish restricted candidate scope or sparse
physical execution. In \DAWN{},
\[
\mathcal{I}_a(x)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N_a\},
\]
so the candidate scope is the full type-specific operator pool and physical RW
execution is dense. Applicability support specifies which operators contribute;
candidate scope specifies which operators control considers; physical execution
specifies how computation maps to hardware. \textbf{Sparse
applicability support does not by itself imply hardware-exploitable sparse
computation.} The open problem is to restrict candidate scope and physical
execution while preserving language-model quality and conditional organization.

\subsection{Limitations}

Global candidate evaluation and dense RW execution remain expensive,
and performance may depend partly on the capacity of the full operator pool.
Even static accounting with RW execution restricted to exact applicability
support remains 2.1168 times the matched dense control's backbone. No
production latency, energy, or hardware advantage is measured.

The study does not establish that the State-Conditioned Program organization or
its control--operation factorization is intrinsic, unique, necessary,
privileged, or optimal. Nor are the spherical geometry, \(d_c=256\) control
dimension, or correspondence between geometric and functional organization
shown to be intrinsic; the geometry may instead be a high-capacity substrate
supporting broadly distributed operations.

Causal evidence is confined to the specified IOI setting. Fail-closed results on
RAVEL, ARC, and 1.3B IOI delimit mechanistic generalization; the differently
budgeted 400M and 1.3B checkpoints do not define a controlled scaling curve; and
individual operators need not map one-to-one to human-readable concepts.

\section{Conclusion}

Learned state-transition computation remains organizationally implicit. The
State-Conditioned Program formulation organizes learned state transitions
through explicit state-conditioned control over learned operations and explicit
composition of their controlled contributions; \DAWN{} tests the narrower
architectural hypothesis in language modeling through its rank-1 RW
realization.

The evidence is bounded: measured 400M viability, descriptive 1.3B results,
evidence of conditional organization from state-conditioned exact applicability
support, and causal accessibility through a
discovery-frozen IOI circuit tested by suppression, matched controls, and exact
restoration. Fail-closed results mark where localization did not succeed.

The main interpretation is that one learned high-dimensional state-transition
system can be organized through explicit state-conditioned control and learned
operations while retaining viability, not that this decomposition is intrinsic
or unique. The
resulting open question is not merely whether learned computation can be
factorized into state-conditioned control and learned operations, but when such
a factorization becomes
a useful computational decomposition---and how far it can replace or expose
otherwise implicit neural computation.

\section{Exact Operation-Address and Composition Equations}
\label{app:equations}

This appendix restates the production computation as a compact reference. Raw
read and write parameters are normalized in float32 before being cast for
dense contractions:
\[
\bar r_i=\frac{r_i}{\max(\norm{r_i}_2,\epsilon)},\qquad
\bar w_i=\frac{w_i}{\max(\norm{w_i}_2,\epsilon)}.
\]
One pair of model-level probes is shared across QK, V, and RST. In canonical
notation, the operator address is
\[
k_{a,i}=\operatorname{unit}\!\left(
\operatorname{unit}(\bar r_{a,i}^{\top}P_r)\od
\operatorname{unit}(\bar w_{a,i}^{\top}P_w)\right).
\]
The query and applicability threshold use separate direct projections. The
\texttt{DirectTau} projection produces the implementation variable
\texttt{raw\_tau} \(=xu_a+\beta_a\), whose transformed value is the canonical
applicability threshold \(\tau_a(x)\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
q_a(x)&=\operatorname{unit}(xP_a+b_a),\\
\tau_a(x)&=-1+2\sigma(xu_a+\beta_a),&
s_{a,i}(x)&=q_a(x)^\top k_{a,i}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the \texttt{linear\_angular} applicability rule:
\[
c_{a,i}(x)=
\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
\frac{s_{a,i}(x)-\tau_a(x)}{\max(1-\tau_a(x),10^{-4})},0,1\right)
\]
and padding rows are masked to zero. This is the continuous-valued applicability
used in normalized additive composition. With normalization factor
\(Z_a(x)=\max(\sum_i c_{a,i}(x),1)^{p_a}\), the production composed output
before causal attention or residual addition is
\[
F_a(x)=
\sqrt{\frac{D}{L}}\,
\frac{\sum_i c_{a,i}(x)\langle x,\bar r_{a,i}\rangle\bar w_{a,i}}
{Z_a(x)},
\]
with \(p_Q=p_K=0.5\), \(p_V=1.0\), and \(p_{\mathrm{RST}}=1.2\).
Operator rows are sharded over the model axis; local numerators and local sums of
applicability values are reduced across shards to produce the global numerator
and normalization factor. Chunking changes the contraction schedule, not the
equation.

\section{Compute Accounting}
\label{app:compute}

The implementation shards operator rows across the model mesh and processes
them in dense chunks. The 400M configuration uses one QK chunk, one V chunk,
and four RST chunks. Every query scores all local operator addresses, and every
operator row participates in dense read and write contractions before
cross-shard reduction. RW operators execute in the full residual dimension
\(D\); the \(d_c\)-dimensional operation-address space is used only for applicability.

The applicability-support analysis evaluates 9,994,240 packed-C4 positions and
observes applicability scores and applicability thresholds before RW
application. It stores aggregate statistics,
not per-token operator identities or vectors. Static accounting uses batch 1,
sequence length 512, and \(1\) MAC \(=2\) FLOPs. It counts major
matmul/einsum operations and omits softmax, normalization, applicability elementwise
arithmetic, embedding lookup, residual additions, and communication
collectives.

Static forward accounting is 4.741260116 TFLOPs for current dense \DAWN{},
0.818230250 TFLOPs for dense addressing plus RW application restricted to exact
applicability support, and 0.386547057 TFLOPs for the matched dense control.
The estimate restricted to exact applicability support is 17.2577\% of current
\DAWN{} accounting but
still 2.1168 times the dense Transformer. These are static arithmetic estimates,
not measured latency, energy, or hardware speedup.
